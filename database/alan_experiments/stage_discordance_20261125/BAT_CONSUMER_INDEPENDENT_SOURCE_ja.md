# Independent bat-insect light experiments: model contrasts, covariance recovery and stage definition audit (2026-11-26)

## New source review and purpose

Independent physical experiments were sought without selecting direction of outcomes. The question is whether light changes local insect collection/observation more than bat flight activity or attempted feeding. Counts of acoustic passes, feeding buzzes, and buzz probability are distinct responses. No estimate of actual consumption efficiency. The original Robert (2025) extracted workbook, 108-effect inventory and existing manuscript results are unchanged; formal newly admitted k = 0.

## Rowse, Harris & Jones 2018: 21 British suburban sites, seven insect camera sites

Primary DOI 10.1098/rsos.180205. Original publisher PDF hosted by University of Bristol: https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/ws/files/164355864/180205.full.pdf (printed pages 5–7). Text and screenshots of Tables 1–2 examined. Four randomized intensities (0/25/50/100%) at each site, three adjacent poles treated as one site, two nights per level; exceptions at three sites. 21 bat sites, insect camera observations at seven of those sites on one rain-free night per level. Feeding buzz detection on a random 5% sample of passes; model is logit for the buzz-containing pass probability, NOT a count model. Pass and insect models are negative binomial log link. Weather covariates and random effects vary by fitted model. Site counts and model adjustment sets differ; the coefficients are conditional model contrasts, not paired arm means.

From original Table 1 and Table 2, reconstruct within-endpoint 3×3 covariance from published marginal and pairwise contrast SEs using Cov(a,b)=(SE(a)^2+SE(b)^2-SE(a-b)^2)/2. All five reconstructed matrices satisfy PSD checks. Published point-coefficient rebasing agrees within rounding. The actual covariance between DIFFERENT fitted endpoints is not recovered. Use the Cauchy range for the standard error of the difference: |s1−s2| to s1+s2, and a conservative t6 reference interval (not exact model or design-based CI).

## Russo et al. 2019: eight Italian riparian sites

DOI 10.1016/j.envpol.2019.06.105; primary PDF https://www.parcoabruzzo.it/Pdf/progetti/PNALMpro522-1.pdf (pp. 1674–1675, Tables 1–2, screenshots confirmed). Separate nights for insect vs bat sampling, fixed dark-first treatment sequence, four trap locations per site. Negative binomial log-link total pass coefficient −.39 ±.12 (RR .677). No direct buzz-count outcome. Reconstruct pairing covariance for each Table 2 position using n8 and rounded t: SD_diff=abs(mean_lit−mean_dark)*sqrt(8)/abs(t); paired covariance=(SD_lit²+SD_dark²−SD_diff²)/2. All reconstructed correlations feasible. **Reported total-insect means are smaller than some constituent Diptera means. This unresolved scale/aggregation inconsistency forbids use as raw total counts or combining taxonomic rows.** Retain published-scale calculations only.

Diptera mean ratios at positions 1–4 (closest onshore toward opposite bank): 11.000, 1.908, .729, 1.152. Conditional on the reported numbers sharing the same ratio scale, the equal-log near (1–2) vs far (3–4) ratio of response ratios = 5.000, maximal-SE t7 outer interval .960–26.043, unknown cross-position covariance; not a confirmed interaction or proof of a depletion zone. The total row's published-scale near/far ratio 2.060 [.834,5.085] is held, not admitted. Bat passes are not equivalent to feeding buzzes; the author reports species-specific responses and a foraging habitat spatial mismatch. This program is not added to Robert's feeding-buzz comparison.
