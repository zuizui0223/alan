# Parkinson2020: experimental unit and interpretation audit

Checked 2026-11-20 against [primary Methods/Results](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0240138), sections Experimental design, Mesocosm and ALAN installation, Spider abundance and body mass.

Twenty mesocosms received randomized light-by-fish assignments (five per factorial cell). Each illuminated mesocosm had its own four lights. Fish populations could not be maintained, and the published analyses omitted fish. Thus ten illuminated and ten non-added-light mesocosms support the marginal light comparison; fish-specific modification remains unverified. The controls experienced skyglow, not complete darkness. Reported light levels were 27.3 versus 22.7 lux, measured with a phone application; these should not be treated as precise calibrated exposure increments.

The three spider/web-prey counts were repeated within mesocosms. August4 is the final date; a three-date mean is supplementary, not another experiment. Web-prey counts are standing counts, not successful predation per unit time. The probable spider species identification must not be upgraded to confirmed species.

The paper's +51% spider statement differs from the public-data ratio431/214 (about+101%). Retain both provenance records, not a silently corrected original finding. Body-mass records lack mesocosm IDs in the deposited table, preventing the same unit-level extraction.

Separate computational re-extraction is documented in `parkinson_independent/`; it does not replace human source review or establish wet-versus-dry effects. Main wet-affinity result is unchanged.
