# Boyes2021: experimental caterpillar sweep-net counts

Primary article: https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abi8322 (PMC8386932), Results and short-term experiment Methods. Primary data: Boyes DH, Evans DM, Fox R, Parsons MS, Pocock MJO (2021), *Caterpillar activity under experimentally manipulated light conditions, Oxfordshire, UK, winter2019*, NERC EDS Environmental Information Data Centre, https://doi.org/10.5285/7e7ce7c1-aa16-484f-86b1-90c5b088a64c. Dataset licensed under the Open Government Licence; preserve this attribution when reusing source_transect_counts.csv.

Recovered2026-11-21 from https://data-package.ceh.ac.uk/data/7e7ce7c1-aa16-484f-86b1-90c5b088a64c.zip and supporting Methods.docx. The raw CSV has27 unique site×transect combinations at two farms over nine nights, with nine observations per light treatment. Counts total63 in unlit,40 under LED and57 under HPS. Sampling dates are not perfectly balanced: January30 has no LED transect and February1 has two LED transects. A simple nine-pair alignment by row order would be wrong. Spatial units are distinct, but nights and sites are shared; apparatus replication across lamps is not established.

The data and dataset title specify2019; article and supporting Methods say January–February2020. Retain the raw year and flag the conflict rather than silently changing dates or joining climate/satellite time series. DaysSinceStart also does not consistently equal calendar days since the first raw date, so the reanalysis uses categorical raw Date, not this field. Recorded MinsPastSunset includes122/126 minutes, slightly beyond the Methods' stated60–120-minute range; no observations were discarded for this discrepancy.

The long-term matched streetlight observations in the same paper (47% fewer hedgerow and33% fewer grass-margin caterpillars) are observational and are not experimental lighting effects here. This extraction concerns short-term rigs installed in previously unlit margins. Swept caterpillar counts index immediate availability/activity near grass stems; they do not measure consumption, mortality, population loss, or adult pollination.

`extract_experiment.py` fits a Poisson log-link model with light treatment and categorical sampling date. This differs from the original date random-intercept GLMM and is not claimed as an exact replay. The fitted log-count contrasts are−0.44044(LED) and−0.10008(HPS), consistent with rounded coefficients reported in the article. Count ratios are0.64375 and0.90476. Model-based95% intervals are0.43086–0.96183 and0.63231–1.29460. A separate date-cluster sandwich calculation with small-sample correction and t8 intervals gives0.36950–1.12156 and0.65130–1.25686. With only nine nights these robust intervals remain approximate. They are alternative uncertainty estimates for the same two contrasts, not four independent effects.

Both full2×2 covariance matrices are retained in model_covariance.csv, including the nonzero covariance due to shared controls. Do not mix the model-based and clustered rows or silently choose the one excluding1. Treatment allocation was haphazard, two sites limit generality, and fixed-effects adjustment does not remove unmeasured spatial differences. The study is not a wet/dry comparison.

Original observations, arm summaries and date allocations accompany the derived contrasts. These count-model coefficients have not been inserted into the group-mean lnRR catalogue or leaf-damage meta-analysis; they require a separately defined comparable endpoint set.
