# Owens, Dressler & Lewis: capture and courtship responses under different spectra

Primary preprint read: https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-1171399/v1 (Methodspp5–6, Resultspp6–7). Candidate final paper with matching title/authors: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00442-022-05189-6. Final main text remains subscription restricted; do not label it fully reviewed or assume numerical identity. Publisher supplements were publicly recovered2026-11-21. This is one candidate programme, not two independent studies.

Four fixed forest-edge plots at Kellettville Firefly Farm, Pennsylvania, received dark, blue, amber or red lamps in a nightly randomized arrangement over16 survey nights in2019. Four subplots and repeated observations per plot are subsamples. Models treat plot/date/time dependence explicitly. Male Photinus carolinus flash-pattern counts are signals, not numbers of males or successful matings.

Eight preprint arm summaries are retained in reported_arm_summaries.csv. Mean±SE flash patterns/minute: dark.64±.14,blue.12±.03,amber.03±.01,red.17±.04. Mean±SE insect catches/cm²/day: dark.05±.02,blue.45±.09,amber.46±.10,red.19±.04. Thus the reported responses have opposing directions within the same lighting experiment. They do not demonstrate predation, pollination or population loss. Their SE aggregation units and between-treatment covariance are unresolved; no independent-arm variance, pooled effect or precise percentage response is adopted.

Sticky traps were deployed15 evenings, exchanged at1–6-day intervals, and removed between nightly trials. Yellow traps from the first nine nights were excluded; retained clear traps differ in visible properties. Effort is the author's surface-area×deployment-day offset, not continuous24-hour exposure. Spiders and phoretic mites were omitted. The final supplement FigureS2 confirms replacement between June27–28 and omission of the first nine nights; no trial took place June20–21 or July3–4. No trap-level biological data were recovered.
