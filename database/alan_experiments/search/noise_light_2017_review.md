## Source findings and design

The observational survey of49 frog-calling sites is separate from two outdoor experiments. Attraction used three acoustic traps, nine crossed light/noise conditions and ten nights; treatments rotated randomly across traps/trials. Light levels were0.10,0.15,0.30lux. Feeding used nine field containers, each containing a live frog and20 midges, across ten nights with new animals each night and randomized treatment locations. Six movement observations and final host-contact/blood-feeding outcomes were recorded per trial. Under added light or noise, no trials showed successful feeding;40% of untreated trials did. Data enquiries require contacting the author; no open raw-data repository is identified. Attraction declined with light/noise, while frog movement increased.

## Extraction implications

This is parasitism of living frogs, not plasticine attack, moth predation or pollination. Attraction trap counts and successful blood feeding are distinct endpoints. The temporal blocks and reused locations must remain explicit; neither20 midges nor six repeated observations establish independent illumination replication. The lowest-light arm is a low-illumination comparator, not zero lux. Keep the noise strata separate and preserve shared controls. The 40% versus0% result is a trial-success proportion, not a40% individual-midge feeding rate. A finite lnRR cannot be obtained from a zero-success arm without changing the model; retain a binomial outcome and verify trial-level counts/design before quantitative inclusion. The field design is potentially eligible, pending original-data or direct numeric extraction and dependence adjudication; do not carry the urban survey into the manipulation meta-analysis.
