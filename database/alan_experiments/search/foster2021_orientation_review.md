# Foster2021: orientation versus inferred competition

Reviewed 2026-11-20. [Indexed primary manuscript](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/353561470_Light_pollution_forces_a_change_in_dung_beetle_orientation_behavior), DOI10.1016/j.cub.2021.06.038; Results and STAR Methods read. This is the in-press journal manuscript, not a visually checked final PDF.

Scarabaeus satyrus was tested in outdoor arenas. Urban/rural sky comparisons are observational exposure contrasts; a separate rural floodlight on/off comparison reused the same individuals. Ten beetles per condition each contributed ten exit bearings; these are not100 independent light replicates. Other manipulations masked terrestrial or celestial cues.

The on/off test reported greater directional precision under light (paired Wilcoxon p=.042), alongside heading concentration toward the light. Improved precision does not imply improved dispersal. Increased competition was extrapolated from heading patterns, not measured encounters, resource loss, or population performance. Neither predation nor pollination was measured.

Keep orientation evidence separate from interaction-effect pools. Do not code the entire paper as observational, but do not use its rural/urban comparison as randomized illumination. No finite interaction effect extracted; overall eligibility for a separate behavior synthesis remains distinct.
