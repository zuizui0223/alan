# Bhardwaj2020: activity and crossing, not predation

Reviewed 2026-11-20. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2020.110313
Primary accepted manuscript: https://nespurban.edu.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Artificial-lighting-reduces-the-effectiveness-of-wildlife.pdf
Methods pp.5–15; Results pp.15–21; allocation Table1 p.9. Accepted manuscript, not publisher version.

Four before/during/after trials used 11 physical sites (five bridges, six culverts). Eight lit and eight control assignments are not 16 independent sites: five sites recur. Each trial includes concurrent dark controls, four baseline nights, eight treatment nights and four recovery nights. Four detectors per position were averaged, not independent lighting replicates. Site reuse must remain in any covariance/model reconstruction.

Endpoint: acoustic passes, not unique bats, population abundance or consumed insects. Reported responses under bridges: eight taxa/groups avoided lighting, one increased, one showed no detected response; under culverts: four avoided, two increased, four showed no detected response. These classifications are not effect sizes or independent study votes. Counts cannot be compared across species because detection differs. Model-fit warnings concern Scotorepens balstoni and Vespadelus regulus.

Decision: primary Methods/Results read; retain for movement/activity extraction, exclude from direct predation pool. Supplement/raw data and coefficient covariance remain unrecovered. No finite effect added. Do not infer increased road mortality or feeding from above-road activity. No moisture moderator assigned from structure names.

Independent audit: [review](bhardwaj2020_independent_review.md). Bayesian credible intervals are not ordinary sampling SEs; do not convert them automatically for inverse-variance pooling.
