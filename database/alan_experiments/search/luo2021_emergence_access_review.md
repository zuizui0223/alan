# Luo2021 bat emergence: access and causal contrast

Reviewed2026-11-20; DOI10.1016/j.envpol.2021.117765.

[Primary publisher preview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0269749121013476) describes dark, LED, and LED-plus-stuffed-owl conditions for wild Rhinolophus pusillus emergence. The available description does not establish a dark-plus-owl arm. Consequently it cannot establish a complete light-by-predator interaction without further design verification. The owl is a perceived threat to bats, not a measurement of bats consuming insects.

[Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34265558/) reports delayed emergence and prey timing informed by faecal DNA and insect surveys. This supports an emergence/food-availability comparison, not a measured rate of moth capture, food intake, or pollen transfer. No variance or independent treatment allocation is verified here; no effect computed from the abstract percentages or delay.
