# Van Doren2017: opportunistic light shutdowns

[Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1708574114) Methods/Results read2026-11-20 from its original six pages reproduced in a [government-hosted compilation](https://planesynormas.mma.gob.cl/archivos/2021/proyectos/ANTECEDENTES_ENTREGADOS_POR_ROC-OIKONOS.pdf), zero-index pages384–389. PMC returned a browser check; EuropePMC XML timed out. The compilation contains the paper itself, not a secondary summary.

One Manhattan light installation was monitored across seven nights in2008–2016. Twenty-two shutdowns occurred during five years; shutdowns were requested when visual bird aggregations exceeded1,000. The authors explicitly describe opportunistic, non-controlled sampling. Dark periods are therefore outcome-triggered, not randomly assigned treatments.

Radar density, flight speed and acoustic calling changed with lighting. Aggregation is not population growth or consumption of insects. Mortality observations were insufficient for estimating lighting-dependent mortality. Model-derived disorientation probabilities are not observed event rates. Radar-derived bird numbers use scattering-area and altitude corrections; standardized peak density is a baseline-standardized statistic, not raw abundance.

Hold outside the replicated illumination meta-analysis. Preserve as a separately identified temporal manipulation case, not22 independent experiments. eBird visual counts are cited, but are not the full radar/acoustic analysis dataset. No effect variance was back-calculated from rounded multipliers and t statistics.
