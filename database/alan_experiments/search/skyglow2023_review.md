# Skyglow2023: movement and artificial-prey bites

[Primary Methods/Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10613549/) read on 2026-11-20. Twelve indoor EcoUnits received ten skyglow doses, with two units at each extreme and one at intermediate doses. Two temporal blocks reused chambers. Plasticine caterpillars were exposed for successive14-day intervals; recovered dummies, patches and RFID detections are subsamples, not lighting replicates.

Overall movement and bite-count responses to illuminance were not significant, despite redistribution of movement between day/night and habitats. Bites cannot be assigned to day/night. They indicate attacks on artificial prey, not mortality of adult moths. Counts of detected tags do not establish population abundance.

Methods states lights switched on at sunrise and off at sunset, conflicting with night exposure; resolve using protocol/code before effect extraction. Indoor scope and shared Ecotron infrastructure with other2023 papers also require adjudication.

[Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8017438) provided movement and bite files; checksums verified. No finite effects added to the main catalogue. Own raw-data support audit is stored separately in `skyglow_sources`; absent dummy records are not zero attacks.
