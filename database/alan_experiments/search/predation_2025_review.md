# Heinen and Lopez Perez 2025: partial review, full methods unavailable

Reviewed 2026-11-20. DOI [10.1016/j.biocon.2025.111519](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biocon.2025.111519). **Not a completed full-text review.**

## Primary-source information obtained

The [publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0006320725005567) describes overnight plasticine caterpillar deployments at distances from LED poles in Freising meadows, May–November 2024. Total and arthropod attack rates increased with distance; slug attacks did not show that association. Vegetation, moon phase and mowing were relevant contextual variables. Data are offered on request. The [institutional bibliography](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/node?id=1836219) confirms authors, title, volume 312 and article 111519, but links back to the publisher rather than a deposited full text.

## Access evidence and unresolved design

ScienceDirect and the candidate SSRN report (10.2139/ssrn.5333649, located through OpenAlex) returned HTTP 403. The public Elsevier article API returned HTTP 200 but only core bibliographic metadata, not Methods. `view=FULL` returned 401. No original measurements were acquired; no author was contacted.

The abstract establishes a distance comparison, but does not establish randomized lighting on/off, an unlit control, number of independent poles, repeated-location handling, or exposure denominators. Do not convert the word “experimental” in the title into eligible light manipulation. Hold design classification pending full Methods; a gradient-only design would belong outside the primary manipulation meta-analysis. Artificial prey marks are an attack proxy, not measured mortality of adult moths, pollination loss, or plant decline. No effect size was extracted and no missing raw data were replaced with zero.
