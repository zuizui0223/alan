# van Langevelde2017: indoor feeding behaviour, outside primary field scope

Reviewed 2026-11-20. Full [primary Methods and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5377031/) were read through the indexed primary article after the EuropePMC XML endpoint returned HTTP500. DOI: [10.1098/rsbl.2016.0874](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2016.0874).

The controlled shelf apparatus comprised 40 compartments in ten blocks, each with white, green, red and dark treatments. Individual moths occupied cups supplied with sugar-water cotton. Four species contributed 115 individuals over three nights. Each moth was observed 60 times; 6,900 observations therefore are repeated behavioural measurements. Light intensity was approximately15lux. The model included individual, species, night and block effects. Feeding probability was lower under illumination, especially green light. No natural flowers, pollen transfer, seed production, population change or field predation were measured.

[Original Dryad data](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.8v212) list `Data_Moth_Foraging.txt` and `Model_Moth_Foraging.R`. Raw files were not downloaded in this review. The landing-page size-code description reverses the printed forewing-length inequalities relative to the paper; verify actual coding before reuse.

## Eligibility inference

Classify as **exclude_indoor_behavior_only** under the fixed field/mesocosm scope. Wild collection of some moths does not turn the subsequent shelf assay into a field experiment. Retain it as mechanistic background, not an ALAN field effect or measured pollination loss. Compartments/blocks, individuals and repeated observations must remain separate units if an ancillary indoor analysis is later authorized. Do not use n=6,900 as independent illumination replication, and do not count species sharing apparatus and nights as independent experiments. No effect extracted.
