## What was manipulated

Wild Acanthurus triostegus recruits collected near Temae Beach, Moorea, during May2017 were housed at CRIOBE laboratory facilities in flow-through seawater aquaria. Both groups received650–700lux daytime; control nights were dark and ALAN nights20–25lux (6500K white LED). Fish were given algal rubble for food/shelter. This is controlled laboratory rearing, not a field illumination experiment. Independent exposure-tank allocation and number are not established by the main Methods; fish counts must not be substituted for illumination replication. Retain as a separate laboratory mechanism candidate, pending the outdoor/mesocosm scope decision and supplementary design information.

A separate habitat-choice assay used250lux, not the20–25lux rearing treatment. Do not combine the doses. Thyroid hormones, growth/weight, mortality during rearing, and subsequent predator challenges are different outcomes and share experimental histories.

## Predator experiment: direct interaction, different estimand

Methods2.2.5 places predation challenges at day10 after exposure. Each circular arena received both exposure groups simultaneously,8–10prey per group, and a pair of Pterois radiata predators. Predator pairs were reused on alternating days after fasting. The stated n=7 is presented for predation experiments, but independent rearing tanks and unique predator-pair counts remain unresolved. Trials stopped at50% total consumption or24hours, whichever applied; none stopped at night. Endpoint duration thus need not be identical across trials.

The comparison tests susceptibility after different illumination histories in a shared predator arena. It is not a factorial test of contemporaneous arena light and predator presence. Arena night illumination is not separately specified in the main text. Predator attraction or numerical increase was not measured.

Relative survival is defined as each group's surviving fraction minus the arena's overall surviving fraction. It can be negative, is dependent between groups and is not a survival risk ratio. Fig.4 cannot be converted to lnRR of relative survival. Raw initial counts and survivors per group/trial, exposure allocation and predator reuse are needed for a paired outcome model. No graph digitization or reconstructed count was performed.

## Reported findings and conflicts

Results3.4 reports7.1%greater otolith growth under ALAN, higher weight, and10-day mortality of4%control versus26%ALAN with90fish per treatment. These rounded percentages do not specify exact counts, tank variance or predation mortality; do not turn them into a precise2×2table or a predator effect.

Results3.5 describes greater predation susceptibility and ratios as high as9:1, but says the difference is significant while printing **W=7,p=0.98**. The latter is verified on the page image, not an OCR error. Do not silently change it to0.098 or0.0098. A maximum9:1 is not a pooled mean or risk ratio with variance. Discussion refers to day2 whereas Methods and Fig.4 say day10; retain the discrepancy rather than equating these endpoints.

Decision: primary read, numerical extraction held for exposure replication, shared-arena dependence, endpoint duration, laboratory scope and source-statistic reconciliation. Supports a reported prey-susceptibility pathway as a candidate; does not establish increased predation in illuminated wetlands, moth pollination failure, or plant decline.
