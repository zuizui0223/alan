# Boom2020: pollination and pollinating seed predators

Primary https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-75471-1 (PMC7591485). Methods, Results and Table1 read from publisher XML. Independent AI source review confirmed the design and outcome distinctions; not human double extraction.

Four Netherlands sites (Lebretshoeve, Radio Kootwijk, Klaterweg1 and2) each have four randomized light-treatment transects: dark, white, green, red. This is the existing Light on Nature infrastructure, with illumination since2012. Five poles per transect and24 plants per transect are not independent treatment replicates. Silene latifolia plants were exposed for13–14days. The4sites—not the number of flowers, fruits or ovules—define block replication. Other Light on Nature reports may share sites; programme independence is not established by a different publication.

Table1 contains16 arm summaries preserved in `reported_arm_summaries.csv` including the original SE and organ counts. Organ counts are explicitly nested subsamples, never lighting n. Seed set was measured in up to two **uninfested fruits** per plant, not a random sample of all fruits. The estimated seeds/flower is the product of fruit set, one minus infestation, conditional seed set, and average ovules/fruit (404±7.78). It is not a directly measured total seed output and may depend on component covariance/selection.

Original tests: fruit set chi-square3=5.06,P=.17; Hadena bicruris fruit infestation chi-square3=81.17,P<.001; conditional ovule fertilization chi-square3=12.96,P<.01; estimated seeds/flower F3,12=.60,P=.63. White and green had higher infestation than dark/red; green had lower conditional ovule fertilization than dark. These findings do not identify a unique pollen-delivery mechanism or prove equal reproductive output.
