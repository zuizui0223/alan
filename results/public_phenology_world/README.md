# Public flowering-status records outside the United States

Sources checked on 2026-11-12. The regional screen uses the 195 region–year combinations in the original 78 regions (2019, 2021, 2023, 2024 and 2025). The stricter screen requires the original 0.05° cell and year, which preserves the original island and radiance assignments. Species names are assigned by unique exact species-level accepted or synonym matches in the project's pinned WCVP. Rows reporting a broadly identified taxon have unknown species identity.

## Sweden

The Swedish Plant Calendar v2 phase dictionary distinguishes pre-flowering (51), sporadic/start/ongoing flowering (52–54) and absence of visible blooming after flowering (56). Phase 55 allows up to 5% of flowers still open and was excluded. The data identify the observer programme through a quality flag, with 1 indicating membership of the programme. The release supplies coordinates and observation identifiers. Repeated species at identical public coordinates may represent a population. There is no marked-individual identifier, no exposure time, and the reporting protocol encourages reporting stage changes. The release runs through 2024. [SLU dataset, documentation and versioned files](https://researchdata.se/en/catalogue/dataset/2022-86-1) · [Stage dictionary](https://api.researchdata.se/dataset/2022-86-1/2/file/data/plants_calendar_phaselist_2008-2024.csv)

In four of the original region–years this yields 1,236 rows from 83 of the 544 species in two regions, including 154 explicit negative records. When restricted to programme observers, 1,153 records of 82 species remain. Twelve of these species have explicitly positive and negative observations on repeated dates at the same reported coordinates. The exact original cell–year overlap contains 28 records of seven species. All are mainland records. The CSV here lists the region, year and light classification of each resulting species arm. This source alone contributes zero complete four-arm comparisons in the frozen cells.

## Australia

The ClimateWatch archive published through ALA contains 93,603 observations, all with a year and all dated no later than 2017. The current public map is a separate, newer release. Its species selector lists 203 scientific-name labels and eight generic “Other” categories. The two exact species-level WCVP matches to the 544 are *Araujia sericifera* and *Plantago lanceolata*. The live API supplied all 27 and 186 public records of those two named categories respectively. The screen uses the public spotted-at date. Explicit “first fully open single flower” or “full flowering” codes indicate open, while “not flowering” and “buds present but no opened flowers” indicate closed. Unknown or contradictory statuses were excluded. “End of flowering” alone represents about 95% faded and was left unresolved. All 7,214 “Other plant” observations were additionally checked against WCVP using the normalized full value in the dedicated free-text taxon field. Forty-four observations matched a shared plant over all years. Unmapped common names and uncertain determinations remain unresolved. [Legacy occurrence dataset](https://doi.org/10.15468/xlx9o4) · [ClimateWatch official map](https://www.climatewatch.org.au/map) · [Public app category and stage dictionary](https://www.spotteron.com/assets/map/config/climatewatch/lang/en-AU.json?v=124)

There are 81 geolocated explicit status reports in the five target years, including 54 from named categories. Three observations from three distinct public spot threads of *A. sericifera* fall in the original region–years, all in 66_11 in 2025. One observation recorded open flowers and two recorded no open flowers. None intersect an original 0.05° cell in the same year. The thread IDs distinguish updates to a spot; individual plants are not identified.

## Further primary sources screened

| Source | Relevance to the requested sampling design |
|---|---|
| Greenland Ecosystem Monitoring, Zackenberg | Permanent plots measure open flowers repeatedly, including recent years. The station is in 5° region 31_32, outside the 78 regions. [Flowering dataset](https://data.g-e-m.dk/datasets/10.17897/JSQ7-6355) · [Official site location](https://zackenberg.dk/about) |
| Japan Meteorological Agency and National Institute for Environmental Studies | JMA publishes dates of phenological events, including flowering and full bloom of reference trees. NIES recommends frequent field visits and publishes reported event dates. The official sources reviewed supply no reconstructable series of open/closed visits with monitoring through the end of flowering. [JMA records](https://www.data.jma.go.jp/sakura/data/) · [NIES monitoring programme](https://adaptation-platform.nies.go.jp/ccca/monitoring/phenology/) |
| Switzerland, MeteoSwiss | The station CSV value is a date at which a particular phenophase was observed. The network publishes phenophase dates for 26 species. A date field for an event does not establish absence on the other days. [Official definitions and data format](https://opendatadocs.meteoswiss.ch/a-data-groundbased/a9-phenological-observations) |
| Pan-European Phenological Database | The catalogue harmonizes discrete phenophase records across programmes. For Sweden the underlying visit/stage table was used directly, avoiding duplicate records. [PEP725 dataset](https://pep725.eu/dataset/) |
| UK Track a Tree | Volunteers tracked woodland trees and the flowering of plants beneath them. The project ran from 2013 to 2015, preceding all five target years. [Woodland Trust project account](https://naturescalendar.woodlandtrust.org.uk/blog/2021/wood-anemones-when-do-they-start-flowering/) |
| UK Pollinator Monitoring Scheme FIT Counts | A 10-minute count follows a patch chosen for its flowers. This provides flower-visitor observations conditional on flowers being available. It supplies no repeated nonflowering denominator for the chosen plants. [Official survey guidance](https://ukpoms.org.uk/sites/default/files/pdf/FIT%20Count%20survey%20guidance%20v7.pdf) |
