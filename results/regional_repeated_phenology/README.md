# Repeated open-flower monitoring across the fixed island–mainland light comparison

The historical primary response is the fraction of photographed plant species with a documented moth visit. Its frozen island-minus-mainland light coefficient is −1.902 percentage points per log(1 + VIIRS). The 64-species pollen-limitation result is secondary. The endpoint below is the seasonal timing of open flowers. No new estimate replaces either historical result.

## Geography and exposure

Start with all 544 shared WCVP species, 78 five-degree regions, and the 195 region-years contributing to the original comparison in 2019, 2021, 2023, 2024 and 2025. Admit external monitoring locations in those region-years even when the original photo cell differs. Classify each point on the original GSHHG land polygons, excluding ambiguously assigned points and inland water. Sample the same VIIRS product at the monitoring coordinates for January, April, July and October of its observation year. Each month's radiance requires at least two cloud-free observations and each year's median needs three valid months. A monitoring site is bright if its VIIRS median exceeds the original photo site-year median of its region-year, and dark otherwise. The terms describe relative exposure at satellite scale. Independent exact-cell tables require matching original cell, year and physical landmass and use the original cell's light class. [VIIRS source service](https://di-lawimagery1.img.arcgis.com/arcgis/rest/services/NighttimeLightsMDNB/ImageServer)

## Observations and overlap

The three sources supplied 82,369 records with a dated binary status and a usable land/light assignment, covering 169 of the 544 species, 17 of the 78 regions and 53 of the 195 region-years. A record is a status entry, not an independent replicate or a standardized observer-hour. USA-NPN records marked individuals. Swedish records support revisits to reported coordinates and ClimateWatch supports repeat spot threads. Neither identifies individual plants. All Swedish observer categories were included, without expanding Sweden's 2008–2024 release to 2025. NEON's accessible 2019 sample adds no eligible record. Its plot inventory in the region-years contains only mainland sites. Other monthly individual records remain unchecked. Event-date archives and surveys conditional on visible flowers cannot supply dated negative checks. The three other arms recorded open flowers from late May to late July. Primula veris in 2021 had both statuses in every arm, but the island-dark positive and negative came from different monitoring locations. Matching just the original physical landmass/cell-year yielded 86 species in 15 regions with an informative record, and no species with all four arms.

The desired response is the island bright-minus-dark shift minus the corresponding mainland shift, in days, within species/region/year. Duration requires onset and end of the same bout to be bounded by visits without open flowers. A population peak concerns the date of maximum simultaneous flowering among monitored plants and requires overlapping individual series. Both comparisons fail already at the repeated-both-states step. No season length, peak shift or interval estimate is reported. ## Connection to predation

Within the five candidate species/region-years, 72 monitored 0.05-degree cell-year/landmass combinations were checked against the existing GBIF positive records for two house geckos, Mantis religiosa, Argiope bruennichi and Vespa velutina. The original GBIF search footprints and precision filters were retained. Of 15 qualifying animal records across the candidate cells, all 15 have a verified calendar date. Seven cell-years had both a monitored open flower and a predator occurrence during the same year. None coincided on an open-flower observation date or fell inside the confirmed open portion of a bounded flower bout of the candidate plant. Some cells include more than one predator. The positive annual matches involve A. bruennichi and V. velutina. Sampling intensity and false absences prevent converting these counts to predation rates. See the group-specific overlap and data provenance. [Overlap](predator_overlap.csv) · [Frozen predator data](../../docs/ALAN_MOTH_PREDATORS_PRIMARY_FIVEYEAR_20261111.md)

The proposed link is seasonal coincidence of flowers, effective adult moth visitors and their predators. A lit site can attract or expose moths while its flowers receive fewer visits. In one field experiment, moths made up 5.91% of Cape serotine bat diet by volume in unlit conditions versus 35.42% under artificial light. In a separate meadow experiment, lighting reduced nocturnal flower visits by 62% and fruit set of a focal plant by 13%. Measuring the intermediate step locally requires a denominator such as observed adult moth encounters per flower-hour, attacks per encounter and successful captures per attack. Flower availability, pollen deposition and pollen deposited by visiting predators would distinguish competing paths. The two experiments and our co-occurrences cannot establish mediated effects in these 78 regions. [Minnaar et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2664.12381) · [Knop et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature23288)
