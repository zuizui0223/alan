# Bat field studies and related mammalian field surveillance

No verified data source below supplies a count of attacks on identified free-living adult moths or successful captures of those moths together with exposure time or the number examined in a retained region-year. Field prey abundance, acoustic attack attempts on unidentified insects and counts of individuals engaged in foraging each have different denominators.

| Source | Public numerator and denominator | Match to the original design |
|---|---|---|
| Corcoran & Conner, Arizona | Infrared tracking and microphones measured wild bats attacking live control and experimental tiger moths and the outcome of attacked moths. Related July 2010 trials explicitly scored attacks on the tethered target versus a neighbouring moth. | The capture study sampled August 2010 and July 2011, both outside 2019, 2021, 2023, 2024 and 2025. The July 2010 Portal-area site is in block 14_24, absent from the frozen 78. [Capture study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23175526/) · [Related open field methods](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0063609) |
| Fullard, Kauaʻi | The observer collected moths at a security light and moth wings discarded by hunting Hawaiian hoary bats. The assemblage of surviving moths provided a comparison for the assemblage inferred from wing remains. | Island field observations predate publication in 2001. Distinct wing remains and sampled survivors quantify selection, with capture inferred from remains. [Study and tables](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2001.1664) |
| Hawkes et al., Pass of Bujaruelo | Nightly light-trap counts and acoustic feeding buzzes during the September 2019 bat survey. The authors made three years of insect collections. The two supplementary tables contain 14 and 62 rows including headers, summarizing trapped insects by family and species respectively. They contain no individual prey identities for the acoustic attacks. | The pass west of the prime meridian lies in 35_26, absent from the frozen regions. No local light sources other than the researchers’ trap were reported. [Study](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.230151) · [Original supplement inspected](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.23798319.v1) |
| Contrasting laboratory and field outcomes of bat–moth interactions | Dryad Table S1 records capture attempts and outcomes for five-minute trials with tethered moths in a recording room. Field Table S3 records moth-trap counts and trait information. Table S4 reports fecal DNA frequencies and relative read abundances. | The capture trials have no ambient field exposure. Moths in the field traps were not the individually followed moths attacked by the bats. The public file inventory and author data dictionary were inspected. Individual CSV downloads required authentication on the API and returned HTTP 403 on the public download route, so numeric trial counts were not independently reproduced. [Repository metadata](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.4mw6m90fr) · [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/mec.17150) |
| Townsend's big-eared bat pursuit experiments | The paper reports 226 interactions with 180 aerial captures, nine moth escapes, nine wall gleans and 28 aborted pursuits. A subset of 44 successful captures was digitized. | The pursuit experiments used an enclosure with experimentally introduced prey. These observations support a mechanism and do not measure a site-level response to ambient light. [Study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10291723/) |
| Global anti-bat moth sound assays | Individuals were tested against playback of recorded attacks. The outcome was defensive ultrasound. | The assay has tested-moth denominators but no attacks by free-ranging predators during playback. [Study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9231501/) |
| Glacier National Park, Montana, grizzly–army cutworm moth monitoring | The 2019–2021 public data inventory separates army cutworm moth detection, bear foraging sign, and aerial observations of bear behaviour. These are detections of feeding patches or predators. Independent Yellowstone observation flights also explicitly calculate bears per survey hour. | The Glacier study includes two target years, but the Montana park lies within block 13_27, absent from the frozen regions. Individual moths caught or examined for attack are not the measured outcome of the bear surveys. The Glacier archive anonymizes geography. [Glacier dataset](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.0gb5mkmgt) · [Yellowstone 2023 monitoring denominator](https://igbconline.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Yellowstone-Grizzly-Bear-Investigations-2023-IGBST-Annual-Report-508S.pdf) |
| Minnaar et al., South African lighting experiment | The authors compared the percentage of diet volume attributable to moths under dark and light conditions, with ambient surveys of insect availability. | Completed before 2015 publication and measures diet composition. A change in moth percentage could come from changed intake of moths, changed intake of other prey or both. [Field study](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2664.12381) |

The three block checks used distinct region5 and year values in the private frozen universe.parquet. No geographically ineligible study was assigned to the nearest retained region. The geographic search covered terms for wild or free-flying bats, moth capture success, tethered field moths, ambient illumination, infrared filming, migrant moths, pest outbreaks and island studies. Targeted searches and inspected archives do not exhaust worldwide unpublished or unindexed data.
