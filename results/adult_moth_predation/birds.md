# Bird predation studies and a target-year camera-trap comparison

## Real adult moths already immobilized in traps

Bourougaaoui et al. (author manuscript, October 2026) report image evidence of feeding on pheromone-caught male pine processionary moths (*Thaumetopoea pityocampa*) during 2025. An animal on the sticky surface qualified only when accompanied by signs such as feeding or prey removal. The protocol cannot establish that a free-flying moth was attacked, how the trapped moth died, or whether unobserved episodes occurred between the three images each night. The authors assessed predation in Italy and Portugal. The other two countries contribute trap catch only. [Original manuscript, Table 1, methods and results](https://doi.org/10.12688/openreseurope.25175.1) · [Zenodo workbook and raw trap files](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22735263)

The Portuguese paper describes monitoring 10 automated traps through the whole flight season. Trap identities of the two ant events and an auditable file of attack decisions for all dates are missing. An explicit site-specific zero would be needed to compare the two mainland sites. Each VIIRS value is a four-month median for the published group centroid and has four valid months. The frozen GSHHG and VIIRS routine classified all three points. Individual trap exposure is unknown. Zero regions supply all four land-by-light groups for this restricted trap-feeding comparison.

The downloaded Zenodo file compiled_data_all_countries.xlsx (SHA-256 4294e27d94a5f748f4e3c6723fab0154baf302525dc1e1081545a340d2f71195) contains 3,645 rows. Grouped by country and habitat, count distinct Trap code, count day rows, and sum Catch_automated. None of these catch cells is missing. Both raw flight archives were opened. The Portuguese event-level files record image times and numbers of pests. The distributed workbook headers contain no attack, predator or partial-removal variable. The published numerators therefore have habitat-level attribution only.

## Wild birds, live sentinels and visual assays

| Primary source | Evidence for adult predation and applicability |
|---|---|
| [Cook et al., peppered moth](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2011.1136) · [Original presentation and data](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.962262h9) | At a site near Cambridge, 4,864 released, naturally settling adults were followed over six emergence seasons before 2008. Approximately one-quarter of disappearances were directly observed bird predation. The target years have no observations. |
| [Winters et al., wood tiger moth](https://doi.org/10.3389/fevo.2021.657740) | In individual captive-bird assays, 236 of 251 approached moth trials resulted in attack. The birds were offered experimental prey under controlled conditions. Free-ranging site predation and ambient night light are unavailable. |
| [Poloni et al., box tree moth](https://doi.org/10.1093/evlett/qrad071) | The 1,350-prey predation experiment placed 30 adults per trial on camouflage netting in an LED-lit aviary, with bird choices constrained by protocol. Collection coordinates of the French breeding stock do not locate predator attacks. |
| [Nokelainen et al., bright and dim habitats](https://doi.org/10.1093/beheco/arab111) | Forty-nine blue tits made prey choices between wood tiger moth morphs in controlled forest/open lighting treatments. They establish visual responses in assays, with no island–mainland or same-year ambient ALAN contrast. |
| [Hämäläinen et al., Australia](https://doi.org/10.1093/beheco/arad097) · [Dryad archive](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.xksn02vnf) | Free-ranging noisy miners attacked artificial moths in Sydney and Armidale between September 2020 and March 2022. The three experimental CSVs are indexed by Dryad. Both sites fall within 66_11, which contributes only 2025 in the original frame. Counts of experimental choices concern moth models. |
| [Field replicas of *Hyles lineata*](https://doi.org/10.3390/insects16040347) · [June 2023 adult replica data](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.31zcrjdx5) | Trial denominators and attacks on adult-shaped plasticine prey are available. Actual adult moth attacks or captures were not measured. |
| [Global insect antipredator coloration](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.8931zcs0j) | The 15,018 prey at 21 sites on six continents were paper moths with mealworms. This cross-continent experiment estimates attack on an artificial prey presentation. |
| [Annual autumnal moth field trials](https://doi.org/10.1111/een.12329) | Live female moths were exposed over ten years in northern Fennoscandia, with 29 daytime and 61 night-time trials. The study was published in 2016 and therefore predates all target years. |
| [Hawai‘i koa moth outbreak and bird response](https://doi.org/10.5066/P9CKV8QM) | The USGS release appeared in 2021 but the observed outbreak dates were 2013–2014. |
| [Nightjar field surveys across ALAN levels](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10965584/) | Six-minute survey effort is known. The records identify nightjar detections or behavior, with no denominator-linked adult moth attack or capture count. |
| [Grizzly bear use of army cutworm moths](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/exploring-exploitation-migratory-moths-radio-marked-grizzly-bears-wyoming) | Feeding/digging bear observations and bear locations, including study years through 2023, describe use of moth aggregations. The number of adult moths attacked per observation is unavailable. |
