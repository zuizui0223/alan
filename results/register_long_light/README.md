# MOIS original observation register × contemporaneous satellite night lights, 1992–2024

The source is the project's complete [signed MOIS metadata register](https://github.com/zuizui0223/mois/releases/tag/mois-complete-93209351-original-photo-target-tiers-v1). All 224 disjoint archives (93,209,351 original photo opportunities) were read after SHA256 verification against the signed registry. This is a plant-observation analysis. No photograph was reclassified as a flower. Multiple photos sharing an observation UUID are collapsed within the disjoint source observation tiers and taxon parts. Only dated, georeferenced observations in 1992–2024 with a unique accepted WCVP species ID enter; reported positional uncertainty over 2.5 km is excluded, unknown precision is retained.

A 0.05° cell-year requires at least five eligible independent plant observations. A cell needs at least three eligible years and at least 20 elapsed years between its first and last eligible year. Night light is the cell-centre pixel in the observation year of the [Li et al. harmonized night-light series, v10](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.9828827.v10), using calibrated DMSP through 2013 and simulated DMSP-equivalent VIIRS from 2014. Years without plant records are not interpolated. Grid centres, rather than locations of individual plants, define exposure. The DN index saturates; annual radiance is not a measurement of floral illumination. The window is limited to the available satellite series.

After matching, 1,922 cells have 16,660 eligible cell-years, 1,529,327 plant observations and 20,625 accepted species. Of those cells, 147 span 30 years or more. Sampling changes dramatically over time: median eligible plant observations per cell-year are seven in 1992–2004 and 25 thereafter. Older observations can be retrospectively uploaded. This design does not control observer identity or targeted photography.

For each species use cells with a record in at least one eligible year, then all eligible survey years in those cells. Response is its percentage share of all eligible WCVP plant observations. A zero represents zero records among photographed plants. Require records from at least ten cells and ten 5° geographic blocks. Weight to equal total weight per cell. The temporal model includes a linear time trend, cell fixed effects and log total plant observations. In a separate light model the covariates are annual DN, cell and categorical year fixed effects, and log total observations. Time slope in pp/decade and light slope in pp/10 DN are distinct. SEs are clustered on the 5° blocks and tests use a cluster t reference. BH correction across all estimable species is applied separately for each endpoint. A directional conjunction uses the larger of the two one-sided p-values, with BH across species. Spatial/phylogenetic dependence, land use, changing cameras, source coverage and selective historical uploads limit inference. No population-level decline or causal light effect is estimated.

Of 2,137 estimated species, 3 have a negative temporal slope with q<0.05 and 24 have a positive one. None has a light slope of either sign at q<0.05. Dactylorhiza viridis, Orchis militaris and Lathyrus cicera are the three negative temporal cases. Separate fits with explicit fixed-effect dummy matrices reproduced all six reported slopes. The three have self-compatibility entries; direct autonomous-selfing and night-exclusion evidence is missing from the linked ledgers. Among the 2,137 estimable species, 924 were recorded SC, 281 SI, 166 mixed and 766 unknown. In 9, 8 and 0 cells respectively the focal species itself was photographed at least 20 years apart. Historical survey opportunities in other cells are real but a missing focal plant is not proof of disappearance. No local pollinator shift, island effect or decline of the entire population follows from these fits.
