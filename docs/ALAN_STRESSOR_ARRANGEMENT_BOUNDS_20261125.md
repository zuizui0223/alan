The author computes combined tributary export by summing two branches per flume-week, not a downstream drift net. Design is randomized blocks, 6 headers each with CT (two untreated branches), SS (L plus F) and CS (L+F plus untreated branch). Four weeks; 144 rows. Existing upstream extraction pooled all 3 untreated branches per header before recording CT means. Their original CS versus SS comparison is not recoverable exactly from that summary. This is not a new independent programme.

Let C,L,F,LF be within-header branch means (C is mean of three untreated branches). Let X be the unobserved mean of the untreated branch in CS. Then SS=L+F, CS=LF+X, CT=3C-X, and CT+CS=3C+LF. All branch counts are nonnegative, so 0<=X<=3C. By summing the six header means times four weeks, reproduce the paper's 5,564 drifting individuals exactly; SS=1,790 and CT+CS=3,774. Identified means: SS 74.583, average of CT and CS 78.625. Their ratio 0.9486 [0.8405, 1.0706] uses six matched header scores and t5; **not** the causal CS vs SS contrast, as comparator mixes distinct treatments.

Without assuming how the untreated branches were allocated, the sharp nonnegative-count bounds for mean CS/mean SS are 0.1754–2.1084. These are identification bounds from observed aggregates, not a confidence interval. Let q=sum(X)/sum(3C). CS/SS <1 iff q<0.426590, or CS untreated-branch mean <1.279769 times pooled untreated branch mean. Equal branch share q=1/3 gives hypothetical CS/SS=0.8197, SS/CT=0.7760. This is a scenario, not observed data; don't attach a sampling CI to an imputed control. A 28% excess of the CS untreated-branch mean over pooled C would reverse the hypothetical CS vs SS direction. All block-specific thresholds and scenarios retained. Published model reports CT>SS>CS, independently verified on primary article; does not supply raw cell means and paired variance.
