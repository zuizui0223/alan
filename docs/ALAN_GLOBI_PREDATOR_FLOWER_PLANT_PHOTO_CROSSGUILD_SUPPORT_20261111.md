# ALAN × GloBI v0.11 — can predators provide alternative flower visitation on islands?

**2026-11-11 / Source-backed real calculations.**
This is a *positive-documentation* and photographic composition audit, not a causal estimate of pollinator service, local bat/gecko predation or a field flora census. Previous ALAN results, fd and the island Chapter 1 freeze remain unchanged.

## Biological question and separate evidence levels

1. GloBI identifies plant names with recorded **moth flower visits somewhere**.
2. Full GloBI independently lists plant names with **flower visits or pollinates links from animal groups** that may contain moth predators: bats, geckos, centipedes, spiders, birds and mantises.
3. The same accepted botanical species can carry both kinds of database link without those animals ever having met on one island or during the same year. This is **source-documented potential overlap**, NOT local service compensation.
4. We matched these positive plant-name flags to original WCVP accepted plant species genuinely photographed on original GSHHG island and mainland landmasses, using genuine source-year 2019 and 2024 sampled-month VIIRS and R2024B UC WorldPop.
5. Matching group-level flower visits is NOT the same as linking a specific animal species documented eating moths to its own flower-visit partner. An actor–plant edge-resolved analysis is needed for that next question.

## Statistical support gate and empirical ALAN comparison

A GLObI-positive moth + another animal group's positive flower-attendance *photographed species share* was tested only if the **pre-fit source-support gate** passed:
- >=20 supported island photo sites and >=40 mainland photo sites;
- positive island source sites in >=10 different 5-degree geographic clusters;
- overall >=15 geographic clusters.
Do **not** fit a regression on single or zero positive sites and claim a statistically identified zero effect.

If GloBI inaturalist and trophiCH-derived interaction sources are excluded, **NONE of these six animal guilds pass the support gate**: even spiders have only 2 island/16 mainland positive co-documentation sites and birds 9 island/84 mainland sites.

**Conclusion:** broad global database evidence confirms functional overlap in the *global recorded interaction network*. But the actual photographic island/moth/other-flower-recorded-visitor intersections are too sparse for direct macro evidence of bat/gecko pollination compensation. Even the bird/spider documented-overlap source associations do not show a robust island-specific ALAN slope. The absence of evidence is NOT evidence that these services or predators are absent.

## Scientifically meaningful next actual data collection

- Resolve **actor-specific moth-predator species -> specific plant flower visit** GloBI edges, preserving whether both records come from the same original study/site/date. The prior group-level plant union includes animals not documented feeding on moths; cannot call it direct predator-to-pollinator switching.
- Audit true moth **adult vs caterpillar** prey records. The completed 24m GloBI source yielded 41,270 moth predation links, but only 4,420 label adult prey, 547 nonadult and 36,303 unknown.
- Link species-level animal occurrences at the **same physical GSHHG landmass and year**; do not turn GBIF nondetections into confirmed predator absences, and distinguish naturalized from native resident predators.
- Acquire genuine 2012–2024 annual light with cloudfree source-QA and time-varying insect/plant recording effort before interpreting historical plant replacement.
