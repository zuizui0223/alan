# ALAN: five primary published-study visitor evidence tiers and chronology checkpoint (2026-11-14)

This document belongs to a **new original-source evidence audit**, stacked on bibliographic [PR #60](https://github.com/zuizui0223/alan/pull/60), and does not alter the older historical primary results or main. Machine-readable public paper-level distinctions: [five-paper evidence ledger](../results/alan_real_original_studies_public_visitor_evidence_levels_20261114.json).

## Why this matters

The verified original database contains **301 authentic day/night fruit-or-seed outcomes / 118 studies / 123 plants**, and **92** of those published studies (247 real reproductive outcome pairs, 110 source plants) say some pollinator observations were included. Automated Crossref and OpenAlex reconciliation **succeeded for 86/87 true DOI-syntax papers**, with 36 OA metadata candidate URLs [GitHub Actions #43538019810](https://github.com/zuizui0223/alan/actions/runs/43538019810). **None** of these bibliographic statuses alone is a real local observed visitor.

Public primary sources were then screened conservatively for examples of *what these guild data actually represent*, including papers with malformed or missing DOI metadata rather than silently dropping them. All five paper-level taxa examples remain **not confirmed to the exact same plant-population, experiment site and year as the 301 authenticated reproductive rows**; independent original treatment and observer source-event alignment is still required.

The four categories *visitor present*, *visitor carries pollen*, *visitor contacts stigmas*, and *measured reproductive contribution* must be maintained separately. No all-insect guild is absent merely because none was mentioned in the paper. In particular, the Marques source **does not** support treating the occasional moth visitors as proven effective moth pollinators, and the Kays and Ganesh sources demand recognition of **predator/consumer/pollinator dual roles**.

## Source-year quality flag: Wonderlin

The original Kendall/Nicholson author study table contains a `Wonderlin_2023` paper, recorded as **2022**, and the original-data proxy groups include seed set. The related published article [DOI 10.1111/een.13296](https://doi.org/10.1111/een.13296) says *its bagging experiments* occurred in summer **2019** while its 24h video observations were made in summer **2022**. The original meta-analysis document calls the study a **2023 thesis**, while the later journal article is a separate bibliographic version, so this is a **potential original trial metadata discrepancy, not yet proof that the thesis's 2022 treatment outcome was entered incorrectly**.

We therefore perform a scientifically neutral **single-study omission sensitivity** on the original 301-study historical-PCNL regression. It excludes the metadata-discordant study entirely, without pretending to have recalculated 2019 satellite prior exposure or replacing the author's 2022 field date. The original frozen 301 baseline beta must reproduce unchanged first. This checks whether the result is sensitive to the suspicious source study and preserves the historical full-source result.

If further audit establishes the original exact source treatment was in 2019, the strict fix is to resample **only PCNL years 1992–2018** at the original site—not to truncate an already averaged 1992–2021 mean analytically. Any new corrected regression must be labelled as a separate source-corrected cohort.

## Macro-ecological implication

Evidence already shows that day/night reproductive opportunity may involve multiple **animal channels**, not just moths versus bees: vertebrate consumers, bats, birds, flies, ants, earwigs, and hawkmoths. But these five studies do not meet the 20-species/25-independent-paper-per-guild threshold used previously. GloBI data have no event+georeference dual support for this source and cannot fill the missing local community data. Direct primary published visitor observations can eventually link biological channels to reproductive experiments, but **no ALAN-driven loss, guild turnover, functional replacement or causal selection** is confirmed from this screening.
