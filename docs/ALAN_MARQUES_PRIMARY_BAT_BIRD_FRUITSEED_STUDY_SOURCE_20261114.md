# ALAN — second independent primary paper: bats, hummingbirds and actual fruit/seed outcomes

**Primary published source**: Marques, Tagliati & Faria (2015), *Anais da Academia Brasileira de Ciências*, [open primary article and Table I](https://www.scielo.br/j/aabc/a/cSzbHtNkrVKjms8RK76zHMh/?lang=en), printed DOI 10.590/0001-3765201520140340. Original Kendall/Nicholson global-source study key: Marques_2015.

This source is **a different primary published biological study** from the SHA-verified Wenzell 2019 Castilleja population-year source. It is not at the same stringent evidence tier: the Marques paper describes visitors and reproduction in the same original study forest and experimental area, but does not clearly report a separate visitor-event year matching the global source metadata's 2010 year. It cannot yet join to the global source as a verified same-year observer record.

## Original data audit

The original author article's Table I and the global Kendall/Nicholson 1,652-treatment published-data source have matching day/night/open **fruit-set and seed-set** outcomes. Each table fraction is computed from its original printed observed number of fruits and flowers, and is directly compared with the original global author raw treatment mean in a new source-locked guard.

| Experimental access period | Primary original fruits / flowers | Original fruit set | Original seeds per fruit (mean) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Daytime only | 81 / 808 | 0.10025 | 3.8 |
| Nighttime only | 647 / 788 | 0.82107 | 139.6 |
| Both periods / open | 327 / 420 | 0.77857 | 73.9 |

This source therefore genuinely measures both reproduction and field animal visitors: the primary study observed floral visitors during **65 hours**, identified bats including *Glossophaga soricina* at night and hummingbirds during the day, described visitor contact with floral reproductive structures and quantified the day/night exclusion effects. The **original source night/(night+day) fruit-set ratio is approximately 0.891**, from the same two original treatment fractions. That should not be interpreted as 89.1% of open fruit set actually produced by bat pollen: physiological, temporal and treatment effects are not separable by these coarse exclusion treatments.

The original global Kendall/Nicholson compilation labels this source experiment year as **2010**, August–October, from the secondary field-source record, and flags that its published paper includes field observations. The primary 2015 published paper confirms its original experimental forest area and includes both visitor observation and fecundity experiments, but the visitor-event year was not independently established in the primary paper's published METHODS. The spatial geography of the global table is based on place name, not a GPS coordinate provided by the authors. Therefore we label this a **SAME ORIGINAL PAPER / FIELD STUDY AREA** match, **NOT VERIFIED original population-site AND observation-year match**. Do not treat it as a second fully eligible source for a global visitor-guild-by-year contrast.

## Consequences for macro ecology

Wenzell 2024: one original independent paper with up to six 2019 population/year visitor × reproduction matches to the global source, five geographically strict within 1 km. Marques 2015: another original independent paper with published direct animal identity and both fruit/seed outcomes (two genuine global response-metric pairs), but visitor observation event year unresolved. These tiers demonstrate why (1) globally documented flower visitation, (2) same paper/site recorded actual pollinator, (3) same population/year visitor identity, and (4) effective pollen deposition must be kept distinct.

In particular, the Marques data show that the actual day/night window responsible for fecundity can involve **bats**, not just moths. The original source also notes daytime hummingbird visits; the lower daytime fruit set does not establish hummingbird species absence or universal failure. This published functional ecology is not new, but the source-verified bridge toward a harmonized multi-channel 301-experiment global database is a concrete, bounded addition to the ALAN source audit.

No current ALAN study experimentally assigns light at the original flowers; neither Wenzell nor Marques demonstrates light-caused pollinator loss. Both positive fruit-set patterns were already reported by the original authors.
